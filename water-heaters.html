Know what you're looking at

Signs it's time to replace

Water around the base
If the tank itself is leaking, it is finished. Shut the cold water valve above the heater and call. Water from a fitting or the relief valve is a different problem, and we will tell you which one you have.
Rumbling or popping
Sediment has settled on the bottom and water is boiling under it. On an old tank it is a sign the end is close.
Rusty hot water
If only the hot side runs brown, the inside of the tank is probably corroding, and a leak usually follows.
The unit is past ten years
Most tank heaters last eight to twelve years. Past that, it is cheaper to replace one on your schedule than on a holiday weekend.

Tank or tankless?

A tankless unit heats water as you use it, hangs on the wall and can last around twenty years with yearly descaling. It also costs more up front and often needs a larger gas line and new venting. In a home with one or two bathrooms, a good tank is usually the better buy. We price both so you can compare.

Common questions

Water heater questions

How long does a water heater last?

A tank unit typically lasts eight to twelve years. Tankless units can reach twenty with regular descaling. The serial number on the label tells you the build date.

What size do I need?

For a tank, 40 gallons suits most homes with two or three people and 50 gallons suits four or five. Tankless units are sized by how many fixtures run at once.

How long does the install take?

A like-for-like tank swap usually takes a few hours. Switching to tankless or changing the fuel takes longer, because the gas line, venting or wiring often has to change.

Why does a new heater need an expansion tank?

Water expands as it heats. In a house with a check valve or pressure reducer on the main, that pressure has nowhere to go, and it wears on the tank and the fixtures. A small expansion tank takes it up.
