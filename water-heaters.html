Know what you're looking at

Repair it or replace it?

Water around the base
If the tank itself is leaking, it is finished and needs replacing. If the water is coming from a fitting or the relief valve, that is often a repair.
Rumbling or popping
Sediment has settled on the bottom and water is boiling under it. A flush can help. On an old tank it is a sign the end is close.
Hot water runs out fast
On an electric unit, usually a failed lower element or thermostat. On gas, often a broken dip tube. Both are repairs.
The unit is past ten years
Most tank heaters last eight to twelve years. Past that, a repair bill is better spent on a new unit, before it fails on a holiday weekend.

Tank or tankless?

A tankless unit heats water as you use it, hangs on the wall and can last around twenty years with yearly descaling. It also costs more up front and often needs a larger gas line and new venting. In a home with one or two bathrooms, a good tank is usually the better buy. We price both so you can compare.

Common questions

Water heaters questions

How long does a water heater last?

A tank unit typically lasts eight to twelve years. Tankless units can reach twenty with regular descaling. The serial number on the label tells you the build date.

What size do I need?

For a tank, 40 gallons suits most homes with two or three people and 50 gallons suits four or five. Tankless units are sized by how many fixtures run at once.

Can you replace it the same day?

Often, when it is a like-for-like swap and the schedule allows. Call early and tell us the fuel type and tank size from the label.

Why is the relief valve dripping?

It opens when pressure or temperature gets too high. A steady drip can mean a failed valve, a missing or waterlogged expansion tank, or high water pressure. Do not cap it. Have it checked.
