The Main Line

What the houses are like

The Main Line is named for the Pennsylvania Railroad's main line west out of Philadelphia, and most of its housing followed the tracks between about 1890 and 1940. Stone colonials, Tudors and twins from those years are well built and full of original mechanical systems.

That usually means hot water or steam radiators instead of ducts, cast iron drain stacks, and galvanized or brass supply lines that are near the end of their life. Work in these homes goes better when the plan accounts for plaster walls, slate roofs and finished basements before anything is opened up.

Towns and neighborhoods

Narberth, Merion, Bala Cynwyd, Wynnewood, Ardmore, Haverford, Bryn Mawr, Gladwyne, Rosemont, Villanova, Radnor, St. Davids, Wayne, Devon, Berwyn, Paoli, Malvern.

Townships
Lower Merion and Narberth in Montgomery County, Haverford and Radnor in Delaware County, and Tredyffrin, Easttown and Willistown in Chester County. Each one sets its own permit rules.
Water
Mostly Aqua Pennsylvania, which is headquartered in Bryn Mawr.
Gas and electric
PECO. Their gas emergency line is 1-800-841-4141.
Common calls
Boiler and radiator service, ductless cooling for homes without ducts, galvanized pipe replacement, sump pumps and sewer laterals under old trees.
