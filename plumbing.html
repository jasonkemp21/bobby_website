Know what you're looking at

Signs it's time to call

Several drains slow at once
One slow sink is usually that sink. Several fixtures backing up together, or gurgling at the lowest drain in the house, points at the main sewer line.
A water bill that jumped
With every tap off, watch the meter. If the dial still moves, water is leaving somewhere, often a running toilet or a line under the slab or yard.
Brown or rusty water
Common in homes that still have galvanized steel pipe. The pipe rusts from the inside, pressure drops, and pinhole leaks follow.
Stains on a ceiling or wall
Water travels along framing before it shows. The leak is rarely right above the stain, so it pays to trace it before opening anything.

Older homes need a different approach

Much of the housing on the Main Line and in eastern Delaware County went up before 1950. That means cast iron stacks, galvanized supply lines and the occasional lead service line. We check what is in the walls before we quote, so the price does not change halfway through.

Common questions

Plumbing questions

Do I get a price before work starts?

Yes. We look at the problem first, tell you what it will cost to fix, and start only after you say go.

My drain keeps clogging. Is something bigger wrong?

If the same fixture clogs again and again, there is usually buildup or a bad slope in that branch line. If several fixtures back up together, the main line is the suspect, and a camera inspection will show whether it is roots, a belly in the pipe or a break.

Where is my main water shutoff?

In most homes here it sits where the water line comes through the basement wall, near the meter. A round wheel handle turns clockwise to close. A lever handle closes with a quarter turn. Find it before you need it.

Do I need a permit?

It depends on the job and the township. Swapping a faucet or toilet usually does not need one. Water heaters, gas lines and sewer work usually do. We tell you before we start.
